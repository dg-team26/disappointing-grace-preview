                               Dedication

                    TO TRACY - MY WIFE


“Healing doesn’t happen in the hiding.”

Not sure where I read or heard that… But I am living proof that it
is true.

For many years after my business collapse, I thought I was successfully
getting over the trauma. I pushed down the thoughts of failure and
just ran at life Mach 5 as if I had something to prove to myself and
the world. Somehow I bought into the lie that time alone can heal
wounds. Just avoid and ignore, and “things” will be alright.

But my wife, Tracy, never shared that camp with me. Turns out, she
was the smarter one.

She knew that the broken can’t heal without a healer. She knew I was
crushed to my very core but still aimlessly trying to piece together the
wreckage on my own. She hurt for me and she hurt with me.

I guess that’s what real love does.

While she waited, she prayed for me to realize I needed to face my
demons before my wounds could heal.

So babe, this book is dedicated to you. I owe you everything.

               You could have left but you chose to stay.

              You could have screamed but you stayed soft.

You could have buckled under the pressure, but you somehow stayed
            strong. You are the true hero in all of this.

May this book help change lives the way you helped change mine.

I love you.

                     I Was There




I have had breakfast with Dave Fleet almost every month for the
  past twenty years.

We talk about hunting, fishing, our kids, our faith. Dave is one of
the most straightforward, genuinely caring people I know. He is also
one of the most honest — which is exactly why I think you should
read this book.

I was there when his world fell apart.

I watched a man who had built something significant see it collapse
under him. I watched him walk through shame, financial ruin, and
a legal nightmare that threatened to take everything — including his
family’s future. And I watched him wrestle with God in a way that
most people only do in private, if they do it at all.

Here is what I never saw: I never saw Dave walk away.

He was angry. He had questions he couldn’t answer. There were sea-

                           Disappointing Grace



sons where I am certain his prayers felt like they were bouncing off
the ceiling. But he never stopped believing that God was somewhere
in the wreckage — even when he couldn’t find Him.

That is the book you are holding.

There is a hymn most of us know by heart:

                 “Amazing grace! How sweet the sound

                      That saved a wretch like me!

                   I once was lost, but now am found;

                       Was blind, but now I see.”

                                                      — John Newton, 1779

What fewer people remember is the third verse:

               “Through many dangers, toils and snares,

                          I have already come;

                 ‘tis grace has brought me safe thus far,

                     and grace will lead me home.”

John Newton — the man who wrote the most famous song about
grace ever written — didn’t describe grace as a shield that keeps the
hard things away. He described it as the thing that leads you through
them. Safely. Eventually. Home.

That distinction matters enormously when you are in the middle of
a storm and wondering why God isn’t stopping it.

Dave didn’t write this book from a comfortable distance. He wrote it
from inside the fire. And the title he chose — Disappointing Grace —
is not a theological statement. It is an honest one. It is the admission

most of us are afraid to make out loud: that there are moments when
God’s grace feels like it isn’t enough. That we expected more. That we
prayed and begged and believed — and the answer we needed never
came the way we wanted it to.

If you have never felt that way, this book may not be for you.

But if you have — if you are sitting right now in a season where
God feels distant, silent, or simply not doing what you know He is
capable of doing — then I want you to know something before you
read a single chapter.

You are not alone. Your doubt does not disqualify you. And the God
you are frustrated with? He can handle it.

Dave Fleet is not a theologian. He is not writing to impress you with
scholarship or correct your doctrine. He is writing because he made
it through something that should have broken him, and he genuinely
cannot keep it to himself.

I believe God will meet you in these pages. I believe He will speak into
your disappointment in ways that surprise you. And I believe that by
the time you finish this short, honest book, you will find — as John
Newton did, as Dave did, as I have in my own hard seasons — that
grace has been present all along.

It just doesn’t always look the way we expected.

Keep reading.



Rev. Dr. Matthew Pickering

       Why You Need This Book




I vowed to never write another book. Then “life” happened. And it
  almost destroyed me.

When I finally made it out the other side of the gauntlet, I knew at
some point I needed to talk about the dark, doubting thoughts I had
survived… ones that nearly everyone has at some point in their life.

These are the hard things most of us try to hide and even deny are
festering inside us. But this is what I know…. Those haunting thoughts
are indeed there. And in our weakest moments they scream at us
through the broken silence. And that silence is deafening.

I welcome you to the journey. Welcome to Disappointing Grace. Crack
it open and come along with me. It might not be like anything you
have ever read. That’s on purpose. I didn’t want it to be.

If you never expected life to be as hard as yours is right now, this
short read will lead you to the medicine you need.

                   My Confession




“D       ad, have you got a minute?” It seems over the years, my
         serious Dad/son conversations started this way. I think my
father learned that I don’t often bypass small talk to get right to the
heart of the matter. So this particular morning, I am sure from his
Florida home he sat up a little straighter, put his book down on his
lap, and readied himself for a “heart pour” from his second son. And
I did not disappoint.

Life was pulling me through a meat grinder.

Have you ever been there? Are you there now? If so, keep reading
or listening, my friend. Not because misery loves company. In fact,
nothing could be further from the truth. When we are miserable
we don’t want like-minded, “woe is me” companionship. We want a
corner on the sympathy market all to ourselves. We want to whine
and spill our guts and have all listeners feel sorry for us.

And that was me on this hopeless morning.

                           Disappointing Grace



In short, my business of fifteen years had collapsed and I was about
to lose everything, partly because of my own decisions and partly
due to factors out of my control. Who or what was to blame didn’t
matter anymore.

When you are truly desperate, the future becomes the present and the
present becomes the future. My ship was going down and I was the
only person on the planet who knew it. Here I was in my mid-forties
and about to be broke. And I had to tell my wife that our family was
about to get hit by a wrecking ball.

I needed advice. I needed support. I needed someone, anyone, to
tell me they still believed in me. I needed my Dad. I had alluded to
impending business issues in other chats with him over the last few
months. But this was different and my father sensed it.

Dad listened without interrupting as I told him I didn’t know how to
tell Tracy (my wife) that I didn’t want us to be poor. That I didn’t want
my kids to go without. That I didn’t want my reputation tarnished.
I complained that the legal team chasing me had it wrong about my
intentions. I told him I might even have to go to prison because of
a technicality in the law that I didn’t even understand. I told him
how I was afraid to go to bed because my heart was palpitating so
furiously that I feared dying in my sleep. I let everything out. I held
back nothing. And Dad just listened.

Then, as I helplessly wept to him, Dad softly said, “I love you, son”.
But then he said something that at first actually made me angry.

“Son, this is exactly what I have been praying for.”

Even though well over a decade ago, I still remember thinking, “What?
I am your son and you want me on the edge of a breakdown? You
have been praying for my life to completely unravel? What kind of

                            My Confession



a father does that?”

I thought about saying those things…

But I didn’t. Frankly, Dad didn’t give me a chance. He proceeded
to say how as he had been praying for me over several months, he
realized I would have to come “to the end of myself” before I could
get better. He knew I was a self-made, prideful, money maker and
fixer of problems.

Others came to me for solutions, not the other way around. And no
one ever saw me sweat. Until now.

With confident yet soft-hearted compassion, my father said, “Dave,
after we hang up, get down on your knees and tell God exactly what
you just told me.

He won’t be surprised. And He won’t be angry. I am guessing He has
been waiting for the exact same thing that I have. You need Jesus to
come carry you, Dave. Cry to Him son, just like you have to me. And
just watch what God does.”

What my Dad didn’t understand was that I was privately angry at God.

I had been praying, reading my Bible, asking God to show me a way
out of my mess. I was still tithing and attending church. And I fully
believed that any day now, God was going to just work everything
out. But He didn’t.

I heard no answers to my prayers and I was out of options. I was
weary of hearing and reading Bible verses saying, “Be strong and
courageous” (Joshua 1:9) or “My grace is all you need” (2 Corinthians
12:9), without it bringing results. And to be blunt, I didn’t think I
could survive another minute with only God’s grace. I didn’t want
His grace to see me through another day in the storm. I needed God

to stop the storm.

Like David cried out in Psalms numerous times,

“God, save me… deliver me from my enemies!”

That was me.

My heart was screaming, “God, do you even give a rip about what I
am going through here? Why aren’t you helping me?!”

So let me ask again…

“Have you ever been there? Are you there now?”

At 62 years old, allow me to let you in on something.

We ALL have been there. And if you haven’t been, you will be. I have
lived enough life that I can say with certainty… No one is immune
from despair and brokenness.

We all take our turn at the bottom of the pile. It’s the human condi-
tion. It’s just a part of life.

I know, I know… if you are in the middle of your “bottom of the pile”
experience, knowing others have and will take their turn is little con-
solation. You probably don’t even care, right? You likely have prayed
for God to fix it, whatever your “it” situation is…

It may be you are desperate for a new employer to call with a job offer,
or the cancer screening to come back clear, or the prodigal child you
haven’t heard from contact you.

Maybe you battle addiction and just want to be clean. The list goes
on and on in this messy thing called life, doesn’t it? And let me guess,
you sit alone at times, secretly doubting God even cares. And the last
thing you wanna hear is that God’s grace is sufficient. Because at this

                            My Confession



moment you wonder if it even is.

I beg you to come out of your silent isolation and take a journey with
me. Here is a fundamental question I want you to answer. In fact,
if you want to experience a breakthrough in your life, your starting
point needs to be answering with vulnerability and honesty. Here it is.

Can God’s grace ever be disappointing?

Can His grace, ever, disappoint us?

And before you burn me in effigy or label me a heretic, hear me out.

I mean, let’s face it, at this very moment you are either listening to
or have opened up a short book entitled “Disappointing Grace”. So
in at least a small way you are drawn to peeling back this topic you
privately wonder about.

So let’s answer the question, “Can God’s grace ever be disappointing?”

I will go first.

I will creep out on the limb and answer the question while you cling
to the trunk holding onto a chain saw.

Yes, I have had disappointing grace moments.

There have been two separate times in my life that I cried out in
numbing desperation to God.

I remember one time literally screaming at God that I didn’t want His
grace when I woke up in the morning. I needed him to miraculously
intervene and give me just that, a miracle, by fixing my situation. I
couldn’t, or so I thought, fight through another day.

You might say, “It sounds like you were mad at God. It sounds like
you doubted God. Wow, sounds like you had a spiritual anger issue

melt down.” And my answers would be, yes, yes and yes.

But do you know what I discovered?

And this may shock you.

Jesus was ok with it.

Jesus came into the middle of my brokenness, my questions, and yes
even my anger, and whispered to my heart that it is ok, and He still
loves me, and He never left me, and He will somehow use all I was
going through for good if I will let Him. I just needed to trust, hold
on, and do a few very practical things to somehow keep standing
amidst the storm I was convinced was killing me.

So, yes, in our humanness, grace can be disappointing at times. That’s
why I titled the book the way I did. And that is why the subtitle is,
“When Desperate Prayers Go Unanswered”.

Stay on this short journey with me.

Do I have all the answers? No.

But I do have a few. And those few, along with spiritual revelation,

In the next couple hours your mindset and focus can be radically

You can and will find hope… But only if you want it.

I will show you simple, defined steps to support and healing. Do you
want them?

Today can be the last day you feel alone and abandoned. And you can
come out the other side in a positive place you never thought possible.
